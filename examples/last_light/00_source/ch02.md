# Chapter 2 — The Upper Steps

Mara came back for her gloves. She'd hung her coat by the stove downstairs
and forgotten the gloves were still under the watch book. By morning the
council would have someone else collect the keys. She'd signed her last
watch in a hand so careful it barely looked like hers.

She lifted the book and turned toward the light. That was when she saw the
letter on the desk. She knew the fish in the wax. Her brother Tomas had
carved it into a cork with a kitchen knife when they were children. He'd
stamped it on the notes they passed through a crack in the wall between
their beds.

Mara slipped a fingernail under the flap. The wax broke in two, and the
piece with the fish's head landed on the desk.

> The lower landing pulled away from the wall this afternoon. From the
> river it still looks like there's a place to step off. I'm making one
> more crossing for the mill shift. If you're there, bring the lamp up to
> the old steps. I'll look for you.
>
> Tomas

She read the last line twice. Since he'd taken over the ferry, he'd asked
her for rope, oil and change for a shilling. He hadn't asked her to be
anywhere.

From the window she could see the post at the lower landing. Each time a
wave lifted the boards, a pale gap opened between the landing and the wall.
A person looking down from the ferry would see wet planks right up until
they put a foot on one.

Mara tucked the letter in her watch book and pulled on her gloves. When she
lifted the lantern, a clean circle showed on the desk where its base had
been. She took the back stairs two at a time.

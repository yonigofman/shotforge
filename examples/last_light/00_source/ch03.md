# Chapter 3 — The Crossing

The upper steps were cut straight into the bank. Mara cleared a wet coil of
rope off the top step with her boot and held the lantern out over the water.
The wind pushed rain through the handle onto her wrist. Beyond the reach of
the light she could hear the ferry's engine come down to an idle.

She raised the lantern twice, then held it high. That was how she'd called
Tomas home from the mudflats when they were small. Back then, he'd always
pretended to miss the first signal.

The ferry's bow came out of the rain sideways. Tomas turned it into the
current. A deckhand threw the line short. Mara set the lantern in a niche
in the stone, went down a step and caught the line on the second throw.
She took a turn with the line around the iron post and leaned back until
she felt the boat settle against the bank.

Six mill workers came ashore. One carried her shoes in her hands. Another
paused on the top step to empty the river out of his lunch tin. Mara held
the light low so they could see where to put their feet.

Tomas climbed up last. He had his cap bunched in one hand.

“You've still got the stamp,” Mara said.

“I had to cut a new tail. Kept getting it wrong.”

She held out the lantern while he tied off the stern line. Then they walked
up together, slowly enough for him to wring out his cap.

Back in the watch room, Mara opened the book to the page she'd signed and
wrote beneath her name. “Upper steps. Six passengers ashore.” She left the
book open and slid the two pieces of red wax into the fold. Outside, the
rain had thinned enough for her to see the far bank. She left the lantern
burning by the window while Tomas went to put the kettle on.

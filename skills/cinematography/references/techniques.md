# Technique library

| Technique ID | What it is for | What it needs in frame | What it cuts well against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `over_the_shoulder` | Place the viewer in an exchange by looking past one character toward another. | A separate, visible foreground character whose shoulder or head frames a readable subject and eyeline. | A reverse on the same side of the axis, a dirty single, or the wider exchange. |
| `dirty_single` | Isolate one subject while keeping a trace of who or what is near them. | One dominant subject and an edge of a character, prop, or set element intruding in the foreground. | A wider setup that places the foreground, or another single that preserves screen direction. |
| `matched_reverse` | Shift attention to the other side of an exchange while maintaining the spatial relationship. | The other participant with a complementary eyeline and corresponding framing, filmed from the same side of the action axis. | The preceding view of the same exchange, particularly an over-the-shoulder or single with similar height and scale. |
| `cut_on_action` | Carry movement across a cut so that the viewer follows the action into the new view. | The same action visible at compatible phases on both sides of the cut, with consistent screen direction. | A closer or wider view that picks up the moving hand, body, or object at the matching phase. |
| `bookend` | Recall an opening composition at the end of a beat or scene so the audience can see what changed. | The original composition's recognizable subject, angle, or visual anchor and the detail that has changed. | An opening with the same visual anchor, separated by the beats that make the return meaningful. |
| `insert` | Direct attention to a prop, hand, gesture, or other detail that carries story information. | The detail large enough to read, with its state, hand, or direction consistent with surrounding shots. | A wider shot that establishes the detail or a reaction that gives it meaning. |
